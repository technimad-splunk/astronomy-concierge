# Astronomy Shop — Telescope Buying Guide

_A curated guide the concierge uses to give grounded recommendations. For live
prices, availability, and the exact catalog, the concierge must use the store
tools — this guide is background knowledge, not a price list._

## Choosing by experience level

- **Absolute beginners** want something quick to set up with little maintenance.
  A small refractor or a tabletop Dobsonian is ideal: grab-and-go, forgiving, and
  great for the Moon and bright planets. Look for our travel refractors and
  starter scopes.
- **Intermediate hobbyists** ready to see fainter deep-sky objects (galaxies,
  nebulae) benefit from more aperture — a 6"–8" Dobsonian or a computerized
  (GoTo) reflector that helps locate targets.
- **Astrophotographers** need a stable equatorial mount and a scope with a short
  focal ratio. Imaging gear (like a Solar System imager) pairs with a tracking
  mount; budget for the mount as much as the optics.

## Key concepts

- **Aperture** (the diameter of the main lens or mirror) is the single most
  important spec: more aperture means more light and more detail. It matters more
  than magnification.
- **Magnification** is changed by swapping eyepieces; sky conditions usually limit
  useful magnification to about 50× per inch of aperture.
- **Refractor vs reflector:** refractors (lenses) are low-maintenance and great
  for planets; reflectors (mirrors) give more aperture per dollar for deep-sky.
- **Mounts:** Alt-azimuth mounts are simple and intuitive; equatorial mounts track
  the sky's rotation and are preferred for photography.

## Solar viewing safety

Observing the Sun requires a dedicated solar telescope or an approved
full-aperture solar filter. Our Eclipsmart travel solar scopes are purpose-built
for safe white-light solar viewing. Never improvise a solar filter.

## Accessories worth adding

- A 2× Barlow lens effectively doubles your eyepiece collection.
- A red-dot or finder scope speeds up locating targets.
- Color or planetary filters increase contrast on planets.
- A solar imager lets intermediate users start imaging the Sun safely with the
  right solar scope.
